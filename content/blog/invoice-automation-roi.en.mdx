A finance team of six processes 800 invoices a month. Four of them spend most of the day on three-way matching and exception routing. After automation, they spend most of the day on exceptions that actually need judgment. That is the ROI case in one paragraph.

## What are the three numbers?

| Metric | Baseline | Post-automation | Delta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Approver hours per week | 120 | 80 | 40 hours returned |
| Exception rate | 18% | 7% | 11 point reduction |
| Days of float | 7.5 | 2.5 | 5 days recovered |

## Why does payback land inside a quarter?

Three reasons: approver hours convert directly to salary cost, exception reduction unlocks early-payment discounts, and recovered float shows up on the cash flow statement. Each one is modelable with your actual numbers, not with vendor averages.

## FAQ

### What is a defensible payback period?

For invoice automation, one quarter from go-live is a reasonable baseline. If the model shows faster, your CFO will push back. If slower, the project is probably scoped wrong.

### Does ROI include the retainer?

Yes. A model that excludes maintenance is a model that fails at month four. Include it from day one.
