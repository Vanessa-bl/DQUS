A finance team of six processes 800 invoices a month. Four of those people spend most of their day comparing invoices against purchase orders and delivery receipts, flagging price discrepancies, and routing exceptions to approvers. The work is repetitive, it does not scale, and it is the first thing an AI automation can take off their plate.

## What exactly does AI automation do in finance ops?

AI automation reads structured and semi-structured documents, cross-checks them against your records, and routes anything it cannot resolve on its own to the right person with the supporting evidence attached. In finance, the three highest-impact workflows are:

- **Three-way invoice matching.** The automation compares an invoice against the purchase order and the delivery receipt. Matches are approved. Mismatches are routed to the approver with the discrepancy highlighted.
- **Exception routing.** When a quantity, price, or supplier mismatch appears, the automation decides who should approve it based on amount, department, and policy.
- **Reconciliation flows.** The automation flags duplicates, missing receipts, and policy violations before they hit the approver's queue.

The outcome is measurable: approver hours returned, days of manual review eliminated, and a lower exception rate because the evidence is attached to every routed case.

## How do I know if my team is a good fit?

You are a good fit when three conditions are true:

1. **Volume is real.** 500 invoices a month and up. Below that, the manual process is faster than the integration work.
2. **The current workflow has rules.** Not every finance process is rule-based, but matching, routing, and reconciliation usually are.
3. **The ERP exposes an API or an export.** You do not need a modern stack. You need a reliable export.

If any of the three is missing, the automation will either underperform or require a bespoke integration that is not worth the cost.

## What does a realistic rollout look like?

A realistic rollout starts small and expands only after the first workflow holds in production.

| Phase | Duration | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof of concept | 5 to 10 days | One real workflow against real data |
| Production build | 4 to 6 weeks | The workflow runs in production with monitoring |
| Expansion | Quarter 2 | A second workflow or vertical added |
| Maintenance | Ongoing | Weekly evaluations, model updates, policy drift |

The proof of concept is not a demo. It processes real invoices from your ERP and shows your team the agent transacting against your actual data before anyone signs off on production.

## Where does AI automation fail?

AI automation fails in three predictable places. According to the [2026 McKinsey State of AI report](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai), 42 percent of enterprise automations are shelved within 18 months. The reasons are consistent:

- **Model drift goes unmonitored.** The model that worked in week one quietly degrades after a schema update or an OAuth rotation. Without weekly evaluations, no one notices until an approver escalates.
- **Edge cases are routed as if they were policy decisions.** The automation treats a one-off supplier dispute as if it were a repeatable rule, and the approver ends up overriding it manually.
- **No one owns the maintenance.** The engagement ships, the agency leaves, and the first API change on a Friday afternoon takes the whole workflow down for the weekend.

Pick a partner who prices maintenance into the retainer, runs weekly evaluations, and documents the escalation path before the first production invoice is processed.

## FAQ

### What is three-way invoice matching?

Three-way matching compares an invoice against the purchase order and the delivery receipt. When the three agree, the invoice is approved automatically. When they do not, the exception is routed to the right approver with the evidence attached.

### How long does an AI automation take to ship?

A proof of concept runs in 5 to 10 days. A production build runs in 4 to 6 weeks. These are the ranges we see across invoice automation, approval flows, and reconciliation work.

### Will the automation break when the ERP changes?

APIs break, schemas drift, and models update. Maintenance is part of the service, not a separate contract. Someone watches the automation, patches it, and reruns the evaluations every week.
