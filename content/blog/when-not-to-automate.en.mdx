Automation does not fix a broken process. It scales it. A team with a messy approval flow and an unhappy approver, after automating, has a faster messy approval flow and the same unhappy approver. The messy part is now faster to notice.

## What are the three signs you should wait?

### The process has no documented steps

If the people running the workflow cannot write down "step one, step two, step three" without arguing, an agent will not fix the ambiguity. It will amplify it, because the agent will pick a path and run it 800 times before anyone catches up.

Document the process first. Then automate what the document says.

### The team disagrees on what "good" looks like

Approvers disagree on what an "exception" is. Marketing and sales disagree on what a "qualified lead" is. Finance and ops disagree on when an invoice is "clear to pay."

If the humans do not agree, the agent will inherit the disagreement and apply one of the interpretations to the whole queue. That is not what the other half of the team signed up for.

Settle the definition. Then automate.

### The cost of a wrong decision is high

An agent that routes 800 invoices a month will route some of them wrong. Most of the time, the wrong routing costs an email, a 24-hour delay, and a tired approver.

Sometimes it costs a seven-figure settlement. Decide, honestly, which bucket your workflow is in. If a wrong decision is expensive, your automation needs human-in-the-loop by default, a cost cap on the model, and an escalation path that does not depend on anyone remembering to monitor the queue.

## So when is a workflow ready?

Three signals, mirrored:

1. The process is documented and a new hire can execute step one through step N.
2. The team agrees on what "good" looks like and the definition is written down.
3. The cost of a wrong decision is either small or recoverable with the escalation path in place.

If all three are true, the automation returns real hours and keeps returning them.

## FAQ

### Is this an anti-automation take?

No. It is an anti-waste take. We have shipped automations that worked. We have also seen teams spend a quarter automating a process that should have been killed, not scaled.

### How do I know if the process is ready?

A ready process is documented in steps a new hire can execute without asking. If the current team cannot write that document, the process is not ready for an agent.
