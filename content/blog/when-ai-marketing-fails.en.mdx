A B2B SaaS company spent a year publishing weekly thought-leadership posts. Their traffic was flat. When we checked, ChatGPT did not know they existed. Their content was long, named-authorless, and never structured for AI extraction. They were optimizing for impressions in a world where impressions no longer convert.

## What are the three signs?

### No brand-mention monitoring

If you cannot answer "how often does ChatGPT recommend us for our category question," you are not measuring GEO. Set up weekly brand-mention monitoring. Baseline first, iterate second.

### No referral traffic from AI engines

Open your analytics. Filter for referrals from `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `claude.ai`, `gemini.google.com`. If the number is zero and your category is competitive, the engines are not citing you.

### No named author

AI engines downgrade anonymous content. A named author with a role, a bio, and a track record gets cited more. If your blog ships "by the team," you are leaving citations on the table.

## FAQ

### Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. It is adding a layer on top. SEO still drives clicks. GEO drives citations and recommendations in AI answers. You want both.

### How do I monitor brand mentions in AI answers?

Tools like Peec AI, Otterly, or custom scripts that query the engines on a schedule and parse for your brand. Baseline weekly, review monthly.
